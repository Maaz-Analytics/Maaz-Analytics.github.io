1. Sales and profitability

SQL Server, Power Query, DAX, Power BI

Problem
A paints manufacturer wanted to know which products make money and which don't, so it could fix pricing and costs where margins are weak.
Finding
Industrial Thinner is the third-largest product by revenue ($1.59M) but has a negative estimated margin (-0.92%). Wall Primer (14.74%) and Wall Putty (10.13%) are also well below the 23.63% average.
Recommendation
Review pricing and costs on these three products, which bring in about $3.7M in revenue, starting with Industrial Thinner.

2. Production and inventory

Date table, stock reconciliation, DAX headlines

Problem
The business wanted to know whether the raw materials on the shelf match what its purchase and usage records say should be there.
Finding
Five of the 15 materials have less physical stock than expected, led by M04 (-2.1%), M09 (-1.5%) and M03 (-1.2%). The other ten match.
Recommendation
Recount and review usage records for M04, M09 and M03 first, to find whether the gaps come from waste, counting errors or unrecorded usage.

3. Product and cost analysis

DAX cost measures, weighted scrap rate, cost breakdown

Problem
The business wanted to know what drives the cost of making its products, and whether that cost is rising.
Finding
Material is 89% of the $8.69M production cost, and cost per unit stayed between $21.07 and $22.03 for the whole period.
Recommendation
Because unit cost is stable, savings will come from material purchase prices. Review supplier pricing, starting with the most expensive products: Epoxy Floor Coating ($45.74 per unit), Anti-Corrosion Paint ($30.70) and Exterior Weatherproof Paint ($30.31).
How I check my work. While building the cost page, profit jumped from $2.53M to $3.07M. I traced it to a labor cost per unit that was divided by units twice, fixed the measure, and confirmed that all three pages matched the source figures again.
